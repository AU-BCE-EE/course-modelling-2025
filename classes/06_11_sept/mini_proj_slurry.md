# Mini-project I. Nitrogen loss from manure tank

## Introduction

At dairy cattle farms a lot of manure is produced and it is common practice that the manure falls into pits below the floor in the barn.
Then after a few days the manure is pumped to an external outdoor storage tank. 
The manure contains a high amount of ammonia nitrogen.
Ammonical nitrogen exists as $\text{NH}_3$ (free ammonia) and $\text{NH}_4^+$ (ammonium ion) in solution. 
You know from your chemistry classes that these two species exist in an equilibrium that depends on the pH. 
A livestock expert explains to you that actually the $\text{NH}_3$ and $\text{NH}_4^+$ comes from urea hydrolysis and urea is excreted in animal urine.
This process of urea hydrolysis to ammonia is very quick (within a day virtually all excreted urea is converted). 
You visit the slurry tank a couple of times during the year and realize that not only is slurry pumped into the tank from the barn, it is also removed from
the tank as his crops needs it as fertilizer. 
The slurry tank is open in the top and $\text{NH}_3$ therefore volatilizes through the slurry-air interface as a gas from the tank. 
The farmer is very interested in knowing how much total nitrogen there is in the slurry when he applies it to crop fields, because he wants to avoid over-fertilization, but still have enough nitrogen for growth.
